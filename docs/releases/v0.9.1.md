# WatchLog 0.9.1

**Servers at the Kubernetes pages' level.** The fleet's Workloads page and a workload's page
now read like their Kubernetes counterparts, in the Servers vocabulary
([Linux servers](https://github.com/watchlog-light/watchlog/blob/v0.9.1/docs/site/servers.md#the-workloads-page)).

## What's new

- **Workloads, by server.** Each server is a collapsible group that says how many workloads
  need attention. Services carry their process groups under them; apps that pm2 or supervisord
  started outside systemd sit under pm2's or supervisord's own row; containers sit under their
  Compose project; a row with a failing workload under it comes first.
  - **Columns:** the workload with its kind (Service, Process · pm2, Container) and its
    problem's reason; status (Failing, Degraded or healthy, now also for a process group that
    is gone or a service near its `MemoryMax`); instances; restarts in 24 hours, 0 when there
    were none; CPU and memory against `CPUQuota` and `MemoryMax` (a process group gets its
    unit's); a CPU trend; what it runs (`node`, `php-fpm8.3`, a container's image; never a
    command line); when it last changed.
  - **Filters:** Services, Processes, Containers and Failing, with counts, and a name filter.
  - **Hide system** is on by default and says how many it hid: the OS's own (systemd, ssh,
    cron, journald, containerd, shells, apt…) and WatchLog's own agent and collector.
  - A process group with no process left leaves the list after 10 minutes; the server's
    events keep it.
- **A workload's charts**, over the top bar's time range: CPU and memory against their
  limits, instances, threads and open files for a process group, tasks and disk I/O for a
  service, and its exits, starts, restarts and OOM kills with its releases marked. A
  service's page also shows how much of the time it is throttled and its disk I/O now.

## Fixed

- **Connections of pm2's apps.** Apps that share their service's cgroup (pm2's) had their
  outgoing connections shown as the service's; each app now shows its own.
- **WatchLog's own agent is told by its unit and binary, or its image, never by its name.**
  Another program called `watchlog-agent` stays visible, and is never hidden as system.
- **Restarts 24 h** never shows a dash: a service's systemd restarts, a process group's
  restarts from its starts and exits.

## Measured

On Ubuntu 24.04 with nginx, PHP-FPM, two pm2 apps (one calling the other), supervisord, a
Go service, a jar and Redis in Docker, against a running WatchLog: 35 of 35 end-to-end checks,
among them every chart's series for a service, a pm2 app and a PHP-FPM pool, and pm2's
`mailer` → `api` connection under `mailer`. Screenshots before and after at 390, 1280 and
1440 px.

## Upgrading

No manual steps and no database migration. Install the 0.9.1 agent on your servers
(`curl … | sudo sh -s -- --upgrade`, or the deb or rpm) for what each workload runs and for
pm2's apps' own connections; a 0.9.0 agent keeps working, with those two left out.
