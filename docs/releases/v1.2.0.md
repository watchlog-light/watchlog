# WatchLog 1.2.0: PostgreSQL and MySQL, much deeper

The database pages now answer four questions: **what the database is waiting on, which
statement is responsible, which service sends it, and what changed**. Still read-only, still
the same least-privilege user, and WatchLog never reads a row of your tables.

## What's new

- **Overview**
  ([PostgreSQL](https://github.com/watchlog-light/watchlog/blob/v1.2.0/docs/site/integrations/postgres.md#what-you-see),
  [MySQL](https://github.com/watchlog-light/watchlog/blob/v1.2.0/docs/site/integrations/mysql.md#what-you-see)):
  the finding first, with the workload or service behind it; the **query flow** from clients
  through sessions and queries to storage; six tiles; eight health checks; and **the statements
  that cost the most, with the service that sends each**.
- **Waits:** what the active sessions wait on (CPU, I/O, lock, client), sampled every 15
  seconds, by statement and by client. Drag across the chart to choose a period; the lists below
  follow it.
- **Queries:** every top statement with its usual mean, **"far slower than usual"**, who sends
  it mostly, and a hint where it reads far more than it returns ("probably missing an index on
  shop.events").
- **Locks:** the **blocking tree**, the root blocker first, with its client, whether it is idle in
  its transaction and for how long; the oldest open transactions; MySQL's history list,
  metadata locks and the latest deadlock (its values stripped before they leave the host).
- **A page for each statement and each table**, at their own addresses, with alerts for that
  statement or table in one click.
- **Replication:** PostgreSQL's slots with the WAL each keeps and **when it fills the disk**,
  archiving, checkpoints and `pg_stat_io`; MySQL per channel and worker with GTIDs and
  heartbeat, and the binary logs' growth, expiry and when they fill the disk.
- **Tables, Vacuum and InnoDB:** the fastest-growing tables, unused and duplicate indexes, tables
  without a primary key, invalid indexes, sequences against their maximum, who holds vacuum
  back, prepared transactions, redo in use, risky settings and settings waiting for a restart.
- **The database process** on Kubernetes and on servers: its CPU, memory, disk I/O and I/O
  latency beside the database's own numbers.
- **Five new default alerts:** a session blocked over 1 minute (critical), a transaction open
  over 30 minutes, a statement far slower than usual, a slot or the binary logs filling the disk
  within a day (critical), a sequence above 90 %. Rules can be scoped to one statement or table.
- **MySQL's connections by client** show where each client runs, its oldest idle connection and
  since when; a client that keeps many connections asleep is named as a probable leak.
- **API and MCP:** the panels route takes a statement, a table and a period;
  `get_application` includes the waits, the costly statements and the blocking tree.

What each part needs, what it costs (measured: about 1.4 short statements a second and under
1.5 ms of database time a second per server) and what WatchLog never reads are in the docs and
ADR-245.

## What's fixed

- MariaDB servers without TLS refused WatchLog's connection when TLS was only preferred.
- Below a tenth of a millisecond, times read in microseconds instead of "0 ms".

## Upgrading

Nothing to do; no migration. The new data needs the 1.2.0 integrations collector
(`install.sh --upgrade` on a server, the chart's new version in a cluster); with an older one
the pages show what they had. Services you already monitor keep their alert rules: their
Alerts tab offers the five new defaults in one click. On MariaDB, waits by statement and
"sent mostly by" need two `performance_schema` consumers, named on the page.
